# Should we keep running this ad?

**Who this is for:** the product or marketing lead
**Short answer:** Yes, keep it. The ad genuinely makes more people buy and it does not hurt
anything. But it only really works on one slice of users, so I would put the money there
instead of showing it to everyone.

---

## The question I set out to answer

We had already run the ad as an experiment: some users saw it, and a randomly chosen
hold-back group did not. My job was to figure out whether the ad actually causes more
people to buy, and if it does, whether it works for everyone or just certain people.

## How I looked at it

I started with about 14 million users, already split by a random coin flip into the group
shown the ad and the group held back. Because that split was random, the two groups are
basically the same kind of people, so any difference in buying afterward is down to the ad
and not something else.

I compared how often each group bought, checked whether that difference was real or could
just be luck, made sure the ad was not quietly hurting site visits, and then broke the
effect down to see which users responded most.

## What I found

The ad works. Buying went from 0.19% in the no-ad group to 0.31% in the ad group. That is
a small lift, but across 14 million people it is far too steady to be chance, so I am
confident it is real.

It did no harm. Site visits went up too, so nothing got worse while buying improved.

It mostly helps one group. When I split users into four equal groups, almost all of the
benefit landed in a single group. For the rest, the ad barely moved the needle. So the
value is concentrated, not spread evenly across everyone.

## What I would do

Target, do not blanket. I would keep the ad but aim the budget at the group where it
actually changes behavior. Paying to show it to the groups where the lift is tiny is close
to throwing money away.

One thing to settle before scaling: is a lift of about 0.12 percentage points worth what
the ad costs? My analysis proves the ad works. Whether it is worth the spend depends on the
cost of the ad versus the value of an extra sale, and that is a finance call that should sit
right next to this result.

## Where I would be careful

I built the user groups from an anonymized feature, so I cannot yet say in plain terms who
the high-response group actually is. Figuring that out is the next step before spending real
money on targeting.

This is also an average over the experiment window. A longer stretch, or a different season,
could change how big the effect is.

And "real" is not the same as "worth it." The ad clearly has an effect. Whether to keep
paying for it is the money question above.
